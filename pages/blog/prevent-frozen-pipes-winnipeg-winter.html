Frozen Pipes:How to Prevent Themin a Winnipeg Winter

When it's minus 30 outside, the pipes in your outside walls are working hard. Eight ways to keep them flowing, and what to do if one freezes.

By the team at R. Jacobs Plumbing · City-licensed, BBB A+, serving Manitoba since 2007

Quick answer

Keep the heat on, insulate pipes in outside walls, crawl spaces and garages, shut off and drain outdoor taps, seal drafts near plumbing, open cabinet doors on cold nights, and let a tap drip slowly during deep cold snaps. If a pipe freezes, thaw it gently and never with an open flame.

Winnipeg regularly sees stretches well below minus 30. Most homes handle it fine, until a draft finds a pipe in an outside wall. A frozen pipe is an inconvenience. A frozen pipe that splits is a flood. Here's how to avoid both.

Where pipes freeze most

  • Kitchen and bathroom sinks on outside walls.
  • Crawl spaces, unheated garages and three-season rooms.
  • Lines that run near rim joists or basement windows.
  • Outdoor taps that weren't shut off for the winter.
  • The water service line, where it enters the house.

8 ways to prevent frozen pipes

  1. Keep the heat on, even when you travel. A cold, empty house is the most common setup for a frozen pipe.
  2. Insulate exposed pipes in crawl spaces, garages and along outside walls with foam pipe insulation.
  3. Shut off outdoor taps from the inside valve, then open the outside tap to drain it. Disconnect garden hoses.
  4. Seal drafts around rim joists, dryer vents and where pipes pass through walls.
  5. Open cabinet doors under sinks on outside walls during cold snaps so warm air reaches the pipes.
  6. Let a tap drip slowly during extreme cold if it's fed by a pipe that has frozen before.
  7. Keep the garage door closed if water lines run through the garage.
  8. If you're away for more than a day or two, ask someone to check the house.

How to tell a pipe is frozen

  • A tap gives a trickle or nothing at all, while others work.
  • Frost on an exposed pipe, or a pipe that looks bulged.
  • A toilet that won't refill.
  • Strange smells from a drain, a sign the line is blocked with ice.

How to thaw a frozen pipe safely

  1. Open the tap the pipe feeds so melting water can flow out.
  2. Start warming nearest the tap and work back toward the frozen section.
  3. Use gentle heat: a hair dryer, a heating pad, or warm towels.
  4. Never use a torch or open flame. It can start a fire or crack the pipe.
  5. Check for leaks once water flows again. A frozen pipe may have split.

No water anywhere in the house during a cold snap? The problem may be your water service line underground or where it enters the house. Don't try to thaw that yourself. Call a plumber.

When to call a plumber

Call if you can't find the frozen section, if it's inside a wall or ceiling, if the whole house has no water, or if you see any sign of a split. We're open 24/7 for emergency plumbing, and we handle pipe repair and re-piping, including lines that keep freezing in the same spot.

If a pipe has already burst, follow our step-by-step burst pipe guide first.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I leave a tap dripping in winter?

Only during extreme cold, and only on a tap fed by a pipe that's at risk. Moving water is much harder to freeze, and a slow drip uses very little.

Will a frozen pipe always burst?

No, but the risk is real. The pressure that splits a pipe builds between the ice and a closed tap, which is why opening the tap while you thaw it matters.

Can you fix a pipe that keeps freezing?

Yes. Often the fix is rerouting or insulating the line, or sealing the draft that's causing it. We'll find out why it keeps happening.
