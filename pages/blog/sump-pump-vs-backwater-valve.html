Sump Pump or BackwaterValve: Which Does YourBasement Need?

One keeps groundwater out, the other keeps sewage out. Here's how to tell which one your basement needs, and why they work best together.

By the team at R. Jacobs Plumbing · City-licensed, BBB A+, serving Manitoba since 2007

Quick answer

They solve different problems. A backwater valve stops sewage from the city sewer flowing back into your basement through the main drain. A sump pit and pump collect groundwater from around your foundation and pump it outside. Many Winnipeg homes benefit from both.

Basement flooding in Winnipeg usually comes from one of two directions: up through the floor drain from the sewer, or in through the foundation from the ground. A backwater valve handles the first. A sump pit and pump handle the second.

What a backwater valve does

A backwater valve is installed on your main sewer line, under the basement floor. Normally it lets wastewater flow out of the house. When the city sewer backs up during a heavy rain, a flap closes and stops that water from coming back in through your floor drain, toilet or shower.

In older parts of Winnipeg, storm water and sewage can share the same pipes, so a big downpour can push sewer water back toward basements. A backwater valve is your line of defence against it.

What a sump pit and pump do

Weeping tile around your foundation collects groundwater. With a sump system, that water drains into a pit in the basement floor, and a pump pushes it outside, away from the house. In many older homes the weeping tile was tied into the sewer instead. Rerouting it to a sump pit keeps that groundwater out of the sewer line.

Why they work best together

When a backwater valve closes, nothing can drain out through the sewer line, including water from weeping tile that's tied into it. Without a sump pit, that water has nowhere to go but your basement. Installing both, with the weeping tile disconnected from the sewer and routed to the sump, closes both doors.

Signs you need a backwater valve

  • Sewage or dirty water has come up through your floor drain before.
  • Your neighbours have had sewer backups.
  • Your floor drain gurgles or rises during heavy rain.
  • You're finishing the basement and want to protect the investment.

Signs you need a sump pump

  • Water seeps in along the walls or floor in spring melt.
  • The basement smells damp or musty every spring.
  • Your weeping tile still drains into the sewer line.
  • Your existing pump runs constantly or is getting old.

Keep them working

  • Test the sump pump each spring by slowly pouring a pail of water into the pit and watching it switch on.
  • Add a battery backup. Storms knock out power exactly when the pump is needed most.
  • Keep the backwater valve access cover clear and have the valve checked every year.
  • When the valve is closed during a backup, use as little water as possible. Water from your own fixtures can't leave the house either.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a backwater valve need maintenance?

Yes. Debris can stop the flap from sealing. Keep the access cover clear and have the valve inspected once a year.

Can I use water when my backwater valve is closed?

Use as little as possible. While the valve is closed, water from your sinks, toilets and laundry can't leave the house and can back up into the basement.

Do newer homes already have a backwater valve?

Many do, but not all. If you're not sure, we can check during a visit.
