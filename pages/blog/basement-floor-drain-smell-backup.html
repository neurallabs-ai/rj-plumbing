Why Your BasementFloor Drain Smellsor Backs Up

A sewer smell in the basement is often a two-minute fix. Water coming up through the drain is a different story. Here's how to tell them apart.

By the team at R. Jacobs Plumbing · City-licensed, BBB A+, serving Manitoba since 2007

Quick answer

A smelly floor drain usually has a dry trap, so pour a few litres of water into it. A floor drain that backs up usually means a blockage in the main drain or a sewer backup during heavy rain. If water or sewage is coming up, stop using water in the house and call a plumber.

The floor drain is the lowest point in your plumbing, so it's where problems show up first. Smells and backups have very different causes, and very different fixes.

Why a floor drain smells

Every floor drain has a trap: a bend that holds water to block sewer gas. Drains that rarely get water, like the one in a furnace room, can dry out, especially in winter when the air is dry. With no water seal, sewer gas comes straight up.

Pour three or four litres of water into the drain. If the smell fades within a few hours, the trap was dry. Topping it up every few weeks keeps it sealed.

If the smell comes back quickly, the trap may be cracked, a cleanout cap may be loose or missing, or there could be a venting problem. Those need a plumber.

Why a floor drain backs up

  • A blockage in the main drain from grease, wipes, hair or laundry lint.
  • Roots or damage in an older clay or cast-iron sewer line.
  • A sewer backup when heavy rain overwhelms the city system.
  • A failed or missing backwater valve.

Signs the problem is your main line

  • Water comes up in the floor drain when the washing machine drains.
  • Several fixtures gurgle or drain slowly at the same time.
  • Flushing a toilet makes water rise in the shower or floor drain.
  • The backup gets worse after heavy rain.

What to do right now

  1. Stop using water: no laundry, flushing or showers.
  2. Keep people and pets away from any sewage. It's a health hazard.
  3. Move belongings off the floor.
  4. Call a plumber. We're open 24/7 at (204) 453-8386.

What not to do

  • Don't pour chemical drain cleaner into a backed-up floor drain. It rarely clears a main-line blockage and you may end up standing in it.
  • Don't flush "flushable" wipes. They don't break down like toilet paper and are a common cause of main-line clogs.
  • Don't keep running water to see if it clears.

How we fix it

We find where the blockage is, clear the line properly and tell you what caused it. If heavy rain is the trigger, a backwater valve and sump pit is the long-term fix. Our guide to sump pumps vs. backwater valves explains the difference.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it normal for a floor drain to have water in it?

Yes. The water you see is the trap seal that blocks sewer gas. It should sit a few inches down and stay still.

Why does my floor drain back up when I do laundry?

Usually a partial blockage further down the main line. The washer sends a lot of water at once, and with nowhere to go it comes up at the lowest point.

Are flushable wipes safe for my plumbing?

No. They don't break down the way toilet paper does and are a common cause of main sewer clogs.
