What to Do Whena Pipe Bursts

Water is spreading and every minute counts. Here are the six steps to take before the plumber arrives, and the mistakes that make it worse.

By the team at R. Jacobs Plumbing · City-licensed, BBB A+, serving Manitoba since 2007

Quick answer

Shut off your main water valve, make the area safe from electricity, open a cold tap on the lowest floor to drain the lines, move valuables, take photos for insurance, then call a licensed plumber. In Winnipeg and Starbuck, R. Jacobs Plumbing answers 24/7 at (204) 453-8386.

A burst pipe can put hundreds of litres of water into a basement in the time it takes to find a flashlight. What you do in the first ten minutes decides whether you're dealing with a wet floor or a full renovation. Here's the order we'd follow in your shoes.

Step 1: Shut off the main water valve

In most Winnipeg homes, the main shut-off is in the basement where the water line comes through the foundation, usually right beside the water meter. Turn a round handle clockwise until it stops, or turn a lever handle a quarter turn so it sits across the pipe.

Don't know where your shut-off is? Find it today and make sure everyone in the house knows too. It's the most useful thing to know in any plumbing emergency.

Step 2: Make the area safe

  • If water is near outlets, a furnace or your electrical panel, don't walk through it. Shut off power at the panel only if you can reach it without standing in water.
  • If the water heater is leaking, turn off its power, or set the gas control to off.
  • Keep kids and pets away from the area.

Step 3: Drain the lines

Open a cold-water tap on the lowest floor and flush the toilets. This empties the water still sitting in the pipes, takes pressure off the break and cuts down how much more escapes.

Step 4: Limit the damage

  • Move furniture, boxes and electronics off the floor.
  • Soak up standing water with towels, a mop or a wet/dry vacuum.
  • Put a bucket under the break and open nearby cabinets so they can dry.
  • Keep the heat on. Warm, moving air helps everything dry and stops other pipes freezing.

Step 5: Document everything for insurance

Before you clean up, take photos and video of the break, the water and anything damaged. Keep receipts for clean-up supplies. Claims usually go smoother with clear photos and a plumber's written description of what failed.

Step 6: Call a licensed plumber

A burst pipe almost always needs the damaged section cut out and replaced. Tape and clamps only buy time. A licensed plumber will repair the break, check the rest of the line for weak spots and test the system before the water goes back on.

What not to do

  • Don't use appliances or extension cords anywhere near standing water.
  • Don't treat tape or a clamp as a permanent fix. A bad patch can fail again behind a finished wall.
  • Don't turn the water back on to check whether it's still leaking.
  • Don't use an open flame to thaw a pipe that froze and split.

Why pipes burst in Winnipeg

Most burst pipes here come down to two things: freezing and age. When water freezes it expands, and pressure builds between the ice and a closed tap until the pipe splits. That usually happens in an outside wall, a crawl space or an unheated garage. Older galvanized lines also corrode from the inside and eventually give way.

If you've had one failure, have the rest of the line looked at. Our guides to preventing frozen pipes and pipe repair and re-piping explain what to watch for.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a burst pipe covered by home insurance?

It depends on your policy and the cause. Sudden, accidental bursts are often covered, while damage from a slow, long-term leak may not be. Check with your insurer, and take photos before you clean up.

Can I fix a burst pipe myself?

A clamp or repair tape can slow a small leak until help arrives, but the damaged section should be cut out and properly replaced. A patch can fail again behind a wall.
