7 Signs YourWater Heater IsAbout to Fail

Water heaters rarely fail without warning. Here are the seven signs to watch for, and how to decide between a repair and a new tank.

By the team at R. Jacobs Plumbing · City-licensed, BBB A+, serving Manitoba since 2007

Quick answer

Watch for hot water running out faster, rusty or discoloured hot water, rumbling or popping noises, water around the base of the tank, an older tank, inconsistent temperature, and rising energy bills. A leaking tank needs replacing. Most other problems can be checked and often repaired.

A water heater usually tells you it's in trouble long before it floods the utility room. Catch the signs early and you can plan a repair or replacement instead of taking cold showers while you wait.

1. You run out of hot water faster

Sediment builds up in the bottom of the tank over time and takes up space that used to hold hot water. On electric tanks, a failing element can also cut your supply in half.

2. Rusty or discoloured hot water

If only the hot water looks rusty, the inside of the tank may be corroding. That's often a sign the tank is near the end of its life.

3. Rumbling, popping or banging

These noises usually come from sediment at the bottom of the tank. As the burner or element heats it, water trapped underneath pops and bubbles. It makes the heater work harder and wears it out faster.

4. Water around the base

Check whether it's coming from a fitting, a valve or the tank itself. Fittings and valves can usually be repaired. A leak from the tank body can't, and the tank will need replacing.

5. It's getting old

Many tank water heaters last somewhere around 8 to 12 years, depending on water quality, use and maintenance. The manufacture date is usually on the label or in the serial number. If yours is in that range and showing other signs, start planning.

6. The temperature is inconsistent

Water that swings between hot and lukewarm can point to a failing thermostat, a bad element or a problem with the burner. These are often repairable.

7. Your energy bills are climbing

A heater fighting through sediment, or one with a failing part, uses more energy to deliver the same hot water.

Repair or replace?

  • Leaking tank body: replace.
  • Thermostat, element, valve or burner trouble: usually worth repairing.
  • Older tank with several symptoms: replacement often costs less than repeated repairs.

We'll give you a straight answer on which makes sense, before any work starts. We also handle replacements in tight, hard-to-reach utility rooms. One of our Google reviews is about exactly that.

How to make your water heater last longer

  • Flush sediment from the tank as the manufacturer recommends.
  • Have the anode rod checked. It protects the tank from corrosion and wears out over time.
  • Keep the area around the heater clear so it can breathe and be serviced.
  • Have a plumber check the temperature and pressure relief valve.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a water heater last?

Many tank water heaters last around 8 to 12 years, depending on water quality, use and maintenance. Tankless units often last longer.

Is a leaking water heater an emergency?

It can be. Turn off the cold water supply to the tank and its power or gas, then call (204) 453-8386. We're open 24/7.

Can you replace a water heater in a tight space?

Yes. Cramped utility rooms and awkward access are regular jobs for us.
